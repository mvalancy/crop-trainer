How generation, training and validation work
  1. Generate separate seeded fields for training and validation. Each is scanned with ground included and removed.
  2. Project XYZ into a 5 cm grid. Measure 13 features at each candidate height peak. Nearby labeled centers supply positive examples and far-away peaks supply negatives.
  3. Balance training examples and normalize them using training statistics. Fit the selected network or logistic model by minimizing binary cross-entropy.
  4. Score peaks on unseen fields and retain those at or above the selected detection cutoff. Keep the higher-confidence pin when candidates fall inside the crop’s spacing filter. Match remaining pins to planted centers at three distances.
  5. Field generation and scoring use the CPU. Model fitting uses your selected backend. An epoch is one pass over the training examples.

Which height should I try?

Lower peaks can recover plants with sparse returns. For the original lettuce geometry (v1) with the starter neural model and mixed scans, lowering the cutoff from 6.5 to 3.5 cm raised mean F1 from 0.650 to 0.749 on seed 27 and from 0.575 to 0.704 on seed 28.

Both cutoffs used six training fields, three unseen fields and 40 epochs per seed. Try a fresh seed to see whether the gain holds.

Can a model find plants on a real farm?

In a Sardinian artichoke field, researchers used aerial photographs to find 543 plants in one survey. Their detector missed 58 and added seven extra. Read the artichoke study (PDF)

Our LiDAR test asked a different question. On one vineyard strip, the model placed 21 pins for 18 trunks, and just one landed within 15 cm. Comparing pins with surveyed centers reveals errors that a count can hide. Replay the vineyard test
