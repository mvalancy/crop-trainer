Train & validate

Generate fields for the selected crop, fit a detector, and score held-out synthetic plots on this computer. No data is uploaded. WebGPU training is experimental when available. CPU training also runs locally. Field generation and scoring use the CPU.

How generation, training and validation work
  1. Generate separate seeded fields for training and validation. Each is scanned with ground included and removed.
  2. Project XYZ into a 5 cm grid. Measure 13 features at each candidate height peak. Nearby labeled centers supply positive examples and far-away peaks supply negatives.
  3. Balance training examples and normalize them using training statistics. Fit a four-unit neural network or a logistic model by minimizing binary cross-entropy.
  4. Score peaks on unseen fields and retain those at or above the selected detection cutoff. Keep the higher-confidence pin when candidates fall inside the crop’s spacing filter. Match remaining pins to planted centers at three distances.

Which height should I try?

Lower peaks can recover plants with sparse returns. For lettuce with the starter neural model and mixed scans, lowering the cutoff from 6.5 to 3.5 cm raised mean F1 from 0.650 to 0.749 on seed 27 and from 0.575 to 0.704 on seed 28.

Both cutoffs used six training fields, three unseen fields and 40 epochs per seed. Try a fresh seed to see whether the gain holds.

Compare three cutoffs at this training duration

To compare speeds, keep seed, field counts and epochs the same. Download the settings to repeat a finished experiment with the CPU command in the agent guide. The benchmark JSON also includes weights, timing and scores.

Each field seed contributes a ground-present and ground-removed scan. Both use the same scanner profile and stay in the same split. Mixed profiles approximate scanner patterns, with varying return densities.
