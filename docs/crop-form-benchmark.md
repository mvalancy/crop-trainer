# Can a detector find the same crop as its shape changes?

A young tomato can start as a few leaves and later form a branching canopy. Can a detector trained on a mix of those shapes find both? In three synthetic plantings, the starter detector found none of the seedlings. On the same planting positions rendered as branching plants, it scored **0.834 F1 at 15 cm**. The seedling scans averaged one crop return per plant, and only 6 of 295 seedlings had a candidate peak within 15 cm.

## Run it

Use Node.js 22 or later from the repository root. The first command tests one crop. The second includes all seven.

```sh
node benchmark/plant-forms.mjs tomato > tomato-forms.json
node benchmark/plant-forms.mjs > plant-forms.json
```

Each crop gets one CPU fit. Every held-out scan goes through the same exported weights and Field Test scorer. The height baseline keeps the tallest candidate first and enforces the same minimum pin spacing as the trained model. In either detector, only one pin can match each reference plant. ## What changed across the three plantings?

The first four numeric columns describe the **healthy mix**. Candidate coverage is the share of plants matched to a proposed peak within 15 cm, before classification or duplicate removal. The last column gives the learned detector’s average F1 across 7.5, 10 and 15 cm for healthy plants, then for the same mixture with damage applied to every plant.

| Crop | Plants | Candidate coverage at 15 cm | Height baseline F1 at 15 cm | Learned F1 at 15 cm | Mean F1, healthy → damaged |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Field lettuce | 602 | 93.2% | 0.558 | 0.567 | 0.561 → 0.266 |
| Globe artichoke | 91 | 98.9% | 0.847 | 0.753 | 0.593 → 0.331 |
| Young tomato | 295 | 59.3% | 0.705 | 0.511 | 0.470 → 0.344 |
| Apple orchard | 36 | 88.9% | 0.000 | 0.083 | 0.042 → 0.103 |
| Trellised vineyard | 68 | 91.2% | 0.228 | 0.259 | 0.176 → 0.245 |
| Bench lettuce | 533 | 97.2% | 0.353 | 0.746 | 0.591 → 0.562 |
| High-wire tomato | 192 | 88.5% | 0.365 | 0.360 | 0.197 → 0.209 |

Bench lettuce gained the most from training at 15 cm. For artichokes and young tomatoes the simple height ranking scored higher than the trained model on these plots. In the apple orchard, 32 of 36 trees had a proposed peak within 15 cm of their base. After classification and spacing, just four trees had a correctly placed pin. Generating candidates and choosing the right ones are separate problems.

The named-form comparisons help explain the averages. Loose-leaf lettuce returned 10.5 crop points per plant versus 6.6 for upright romaine, yet the detector’s F1 at 15 cm was 0.362 versus 0.735. Grapevines with two cordon arms returned about 100 points per vine and scored 0.216, while pruned vines returned about 12 and scored 0.244. Crown coverage alone was a poor guide to base location. ## How to reproduce the comparison

- **Fit.** Use generator v2, dataset seed `71309`, six training plantings, the overhead reference layout and 30,000 requested rays per scan. Training includes both soil settings, seeded clutter and scan noise. Plant forms are mixed and all plants are healthy. One extra validation planting is generated by the training pipeline and excluded from this survey.
- **Model.** Use 13 XYZ-derived input features, four tanh hidden units, a sigmoid output and 61 fitted weights. Full-batch gradient descent runs for 40 epochs with learning rate 0.25 and initialization seed `12345`. Use cutoff 0.5 and the crop profile’s peak height and pin spacing.
- **Compare.** Use three fresh planting seeds, `39119`, `53267` and `70657`. For each seed, render each named form, a healthy mix, a mix with about one in four plants damaged, and a fully damaged mix. Keep 30,000 overhead reference rays, soil and crop-specific fixtures. Disable added weeds, sticks, boards and scan noise. Orchard crowns are leafy. Planting positions repeat across comparisons. In field vegetables, the scored box midpoint may move when a leaf changes shape.
- **Inspect.** Each crop’s JSON includes fit settings and four timings in milliseconds. These measure training-data preparation, fitting, held-out generation and validation. Each treatment records per-seed tile IDs, observed returns, plant traits, the number of targets in reach at each decision stage, and per-distance counts and scores for both methods. Pooled F1 uses the summed match, extra-pin and missed-plant counts at each distance. The mean weights the three distances equally.

Two complete runs on a Linux x64 AMD EPYC 7763 with Node.js 24.22.1 produced identical counts and scores. Summed across seven crop fits, preparing training data took 4.06–4.90 s and fitting took 0.44–0.45 s. Generating the held-out scans took 1.80–2.10 s and validating both detectors took 1.04–1.13 s. These are Node CPU timings.

These measurements describe three simulated overhead fields per crop, one fixed training seed and the published generator settings. The 123 evaluated scans reuse those plantings across treatments. Real-plant accuracy needs independent field measurements. [Field validation guide](field-validation.md)
